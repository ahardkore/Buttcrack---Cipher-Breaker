How it works

The alphabet reversed onto itself: A becomes Z, B becomes Y. Applying it twice returns the original.

Hebrew substitution cipher mapping the alphabet onto its reverse.

A worked example

Plaintext:

MEET ME BY THE OLD CLOCK TOWER AT DAWN

No key.

Ciphertext:

NVVG NV YB GSV LOW XOLXP GLDVI ZG WZDM

This example is generated by running the cipher when the page is built, and the round trip is checked, so it always matches what the solver does.

How it is broken

Keyless, so recognising it is the whole job.

History and context

A Hebrew scribal device older than any European cipher, and used in the Book of Jeremiah, where Babel appears as Sheshach.
