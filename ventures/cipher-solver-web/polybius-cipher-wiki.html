How it works

A five-by-five grid turns each letter into a pair of coordinates, with I and J sharing a cell.

5x5 coordinate grid (I/J merged). Both digit pairs and tap-code style separators are accepted.

A worked example

Plaintext:

MEET ME BY THE OLD CLOCK TOWER AT DAWN

Key: MONARCHY

Ciphertext:

11 31 31 45 11 31 24 23 45 22 31 12 41 25 21 41 12 21 35 45 12 53 31 15 14 45 25 14 53 13

This example is generated by running the cipher when the page is built, and the round trip is checked, so it always matches what the solver does.

How it is broken

Unkeyed, it is a decode. Keyed, it is a substitution cipher wearing coordinates, and falls to the same statistics.

History and context

Polybius described the square in the second century BC as a way to signal letters with two groups of torches — telegraphy two millennia early. Every fractionating cipher since is built on it.
