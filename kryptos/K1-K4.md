# The sculpture: K1 to K4

Jim Sanborn's Kryptos stands in a courtyard at CIA headquarters, dedicated in
1990. It carries four encrypted passages. Three are solved and public; the
fourth, 97 letters, is not. The solutions below were re-derived with this
repository's own code rather than copied, and each reproduces the published
plaintext exactly.

Sanborn's tableau is a Vigenere square built on the keyed alphabet
`KRYPTOSABCDEFGHIJLMNQUVWXZ`, which is why a solver that assumes A=0 gets
nowhere on K1 and K2.

## K1, solved

Vigenere over the KRYPTOS tableau, key `PALIMPSEST`.

> BETWEEN SUBTLE SHADING AND THE ABSENCE OF LIGHT LIES THE NUANCE OF IQLUSION

`IQLUSION` is the first of Sanborn's deliberate misspellings.

## K2, solved

The same tableau, key `ABSCISSA`, 361 letters.

> IT WAS TOTALLY INVISIBLE HOWS THAT POSSIBLE ... THIRTY EIGHT DEGREES FIFTY
> SEVEN MINUTES SIX POINT FIVE SECONDS NORTH SEVENTY SEVEN DEGREES EIGHT
> MINUTES FORTY FOUR SECONDS WEST X LAYER TWO

The coordinates point about 174 feet southeast of the sculpture.

The carved text also contains the omission Sanborn acknowledged in 2006: a
ciphertext letter is missing near the end, the key desynchronises from that
point, and the passage decrypts to `...WESTIDBYROWS` instead of
`...WESTXLAYERTWO`. Inserting every letter A-Z at every position, 9,698 trials
in `kryptos_missing_letter.py`, restores the intended reading in exactly one
case: an `S` after ciphertext letter 361. No other letter at that slot gives
`...WESTXLAYERTWO`.

## K3, solved

Double route transposition, following Sanborn's encoding sheets: write by rows
into a 42x8 grid and read up the columns, then write by rows into a 14x24 grid
and read up the columns again. That is equivalent to two 90-degree rotations.

> SLOWLY DESPARATLY SLOWLY THE REMAINS OF PASSAGE DEBRIS THAT ENCUMBERED THE
> LOWER PART OF THE DOORWAY WAS REMOVED ... CAN YOU SEE ANYTHING Q

Howard Carter at the opening of Tutankhamun's tomb, 1922. `DESPARATLY` is the
third deliberate misspelling.

## K4, unsolved

```
OBKRUOXOGHULBSOLIFBBWFLRVQQPRNGKSSOTWTQSJQSSEKZZWATJ
KLUDIAWINFBNYPVTTMZFPKWGDKZXTJCDIGKUHUAUEKCAR
```

Four cribs are confirmed by the artist, 24 of the 97 letters:

| plaintext | positions | ciphertext | released |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| EAST | 22-25 | FLRV | Aug 2020 |
| NORTHEAST | 26-34 | QQPRNGKSS | Jan 2020 |
| BERLIN | 64-69 | NYPVTT | Nov 2010 |
| CLOCK | 70-74 | MZFPK | Nov 2014 |

In November 2025 Sanborn confirmed that CLOCK refers to the Weltzeituhr at
Alexanderplatz, not the Mengenlehreuhr the community had assumed.

What the statistics say, reproducible in `kryptos_solve.py`: the index of
coincidence is 0.0361, against 0.067 for English and 0.038 for random, and
there are no repeated trigrams at all. The text behaves like a heavily keyed
or one-time stream. The keystream implied by the cribs (ciphertext minus
plaintext in the tableau alphabet) is `BKVSBPCXFCTAC` at positions 22-34 and
`LRCFPLJIHUA` at 64-74, neither of which extends to anything periodic.

In 2025 the plaintext was sold at auction rather than solved, so the answer
exists in a sealed envelope. Nothing in this repository breaks K4, and any
file here that suggests otherwise is describing a hypothesis, not a result.
