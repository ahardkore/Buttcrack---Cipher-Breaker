## Latest continuation: four-to-five reversible layers

### Search complete chains, not English-looking intermediate states

A four/five-stage system can remain statistically and linguistically opaque
until the final inverse. The new search therefore enumerated entire chains
**without pruning an intermediate state for looking non-English**.

The first bounded family used two transpositions and two periodic substitutions
in every ordering. A fifth monoalphabetic layer was also allowed at every
position. Candidate operations were:

- transpositions: columnar with BLOOM, BLPNM or NACHTLORN; Myszkowski with BLOOM

- periodic substitutions: Vigenere, Beaufort, variant Beaufort and Porta, each
  using BLOOM, BLPNM or NACHTLORN at phase zero;
- optional fifth substitution: Atbash, or inverse keyed alphabets derived from
  BLOOM or NACHTLORN.

These cipher families and key roles are **hypotheses**, not facts supplied by
the user. Both transpositions use their ordinary decryption directions. This
is not an exhaustive search of all four/five-layer systems or all key phases.

## Latest continuation: learning unknown alphabets and testing dependence

**Still unresolved.** This round tested secret alphabets that were not limited
to A–Z or a short list of keyword-derived orders. No new actual plaintext is
verified.

### Learn the alphabet, then predict held-out letters

The earlier seed-4300 pad tests used only a few explicit letter orders. A
secret permutation could defeat those tests without changing the generator.
This continuation fitted arbitrary 26-letter permutations in three placements.
With letters represented modulo 26, a known candidate stream K, and a secret
permutation A, the decoded hypotheses were:

```text
P = ±(C − A(K))       secret pad alphabet
P = A(C) ± K         secret outside/ciphertext alphabet
P = A(C ± K)         secret plaintext alphabet after pad removal
```

Each case allowed either one shared alphabet or five independent alphabets,
one for each position within a five-letter group. All **8,221 distinct full
streams** from the listed seed-4300 draw methods and raw-word offsets 0–1,024
were tested. Rejection-draw aliases were deduplicated; an accepted letter
stream does not necessarily identify a unique raw-call offset. Methods included
native `randrange(26/52/256)`, one-word scaled/modular/high-bit conversions,
and 53-bit float26/52 draws at both word parities.

Only the **first 6,000 letters** fitted the alphabets. A Hungarian assignment
found the exact optimum of the fixed English **monogram** training objective.
For each input/family/phase-count class, the twelve highest-training-likelihood
keys then underwent greedy **training-only quadgram** refinement, capped at
30 passes. The remaining **2,736 letters were never used to fit a key**.

There were **98,652 actual fitted-key cases** and **72 training-selected actual
refinements**. Including controls and a phase-preserving shuffled input,
460,376 fits and 336 refinements were evaluated. No actual candidate passed
the held-out language, English-monogram-likelihood or period-five frequency
screens. The best initial held-out log-likelihood ratio against uniform letters
was still negative, about −1,379.24. No fitted alphabet is asserted as an
actual recovered key.

This is exact training-monogram optimization plus **local** language refinement,
not exhaustive English optimization over every possible permutation. The
held-out model search is also not a global significance test.

### What the controls recovered—and one error they exposed

Five independent synthetic constructions used long natural English and secret
alphabets in the different placements. Choosing their final candidates by
**training quadgram score**, not by known plaintext accuracy, predicted **every
held-out letter in all five controls**: 2,736 correct predictions per control.

Four also recovered all 8,736 plaintext letters. The fifth had exactly **one
training-only error**, at zero-based position 4,474:

```text
Expected:   ...HADBEENSETAJARWELCOMELIG...
Predicted:  ...HADBEENSETAZARWELCOMELIG...
```

The scorer preferred **AZAR** to **AJAR** in that context. Its held-out text was
nevertheless exact. This is a synthetic benchmark error, not Nachtlorn text,
and reinforces why a numerical improvement cannot authorize a spelling or
source correction. The post-pad alphabet control particularly benefited from
language refinement: its monogram-only key recovered about 41% of held-out
letters before refinement, versus 100% afterward.

Independent checks reconstructed all 8,221 complete native streams, certified
**1,212 retained assignment subproblems** with a separate Hungarian/dual
implementation, and checked 396 retained initial paths plus all 336 refinements.
Eighteen small brute-force assignment cases and twelve independently rescored
greedy-refinement cases also passed.

### An alphabet-invariant cross-check

A separate detector asked a broader question: **are ciphertext letters
statistically dependent on the proposed generator letters at all?**

Within a fixed phase, relabeling either side of a contingency table cannot
change its Pearson association statistic. This permits a screen even when
several unknown alphabet permutations are combined, without first recovering
those alphabets or relying on English n-gram spellings.

The detector tested all 8,221 streams with both one- and five-phase tables:
**16,442 actual comparisons**. It ranked results using `(chi-square − df) /
sqrt(2df)` solely to put the two table sizes on a common scale. That transformed
number is **not interpreted as a normal p-value**; fresh shuffled family maxima
were the comparison.

The actual maximum was **3.74174**. **28 of 32** phase-count-preserving shuffled
maxima were at least as high; the terminal literal I was held fixed. Thus the
actual data showed no unusual association with this tested stream family.

All five earlier controls identified their correct native stream. A sixth
control combined **three separate secret alphabet layers at once**, with five
phase-specific alphabets in each layer. Its correct stream was also identified.
Control maxima ranged from about 65.75 to 154.11, well separated from the actual
maximum. Explicit relabeling left the sixth control's conditional chi-square
unchanged at about **8,381.60727**. A pure-Python implementation independently
reconstructed 130 retained actual/control/null statistics; two analytic
contingency cases passed as well.

### Could the two pages share a pad?

If the pages encrypt English with the same modular pad, subtraction can cancel
the pad. If their pad signs are opposite, addition can cancel it. The test fits
five independent residual Caesar shifts against the expected sum/difference
distributions of two English letters; it does not assume those shifts are BLOOM.

The search covered **7,736 prefix-aligned lags**, retaining at least 1,000 pairs,
and separately compared all 3,660 letters of page one with the aligned start of
page two. The full-overlap cases at that boundary also include 1,416 further
pairs; the separate first-page-only case avoids diluting a page-local reuse.
Both arithmetic signs and all 26 residual shifts in each phase were evaluated.

with 1,230 pairs and addition. **25 of 32** fresh phase-count-preserving shuffles
scored at least as high. Their terminal literal I was held fixed. The exact
first-page comparison was weaker still: −6.82611 for subtraction and −5.48223

They are not probabilities that a solution exists.

frequency fit did **not**, however, recover their exact residual keys: two

**No actual candidate passed either screen.** The best actual 120-letter mean
was about −6.525; the largest weighted within-phase IC was about 0.038814.
Short numerical peaks remained ungrammatical and were not promoted to phrases.

entire direct English message; SHAKE256/ASCII-fold recovered a message with an

### Whole-group numeric channels

Twenty base-26 variants covered normal/reversed/key-derived alphabets, wrapped
I=0 and A=1 digit maps, both digit orders, and three clue-key Vigenère removals.
For each, moduli **2–4096** were checked for an excess of repeated residues:
**81,900 actual profiles**. This would expose simple homophonic channels in
which many numeric representatives encode one plaintext character.

The maximum collision statistic was **4.61579**, at modulus 11 in the wrapped
A=1 little-endian case. **19 of 30** conditional permutations produced a family
maximum at least as high. These permutations retained each digit position's
letter counts. This coarse comparison gives no exceptional numeric lead; the
statistic is not a standard-normal significance claim.

The derived byte channels supplied **23,187 readings**, including modular
shifts, XOR and named repeating byte keys. Of **260,963 filtered short windows**,
one passed the numerical language screen:

```text
"Nb=ELltRee-rd)a "
```

That is not coherent plaintext and is not corrected or promoted to a solution.
A separate short-word screen reexamined the same buffers without a 12-letter
gate: no clue-target word or 7–12-letter dictionary word was found. Direct
modulo-26/27 letter readings also remained unreadable.

A synthetic whole-group homophonic control recovered **all 1,747 ASCII bytes**
exactly using modulus 257. The observed C++ collision maximum was independently
cross-checked in Python. These tests do not exclude other number bases,
moduli above the bound, unknown digit alphabets, or encrypted byte payloads.

### The 36-letter final line as a 6×6 Hill key

The last line has exactly 36 letters, including its terminal I. The preceding
145 full rows contain **8,700 letters**, divisible by six. That is a concrete
reason to test an appended 6×6 matrix key.

The unmodified row-major matrix has rank **4 modulo 2** under A=0 and I=0,
and rank **5 modulo 2** under A=1. Therefore it is not invertible modulo 26.
Both offset parities are singular, so merely rebasing the whole alphabet does
not fix the direct key.

Bounded extensions tested 32 key-layout routes, header-decoded tail variants,
all 26 alphabet origins for those layouts, and balanced +1/−1 repairs of two
of the first 35 symbols while retaining the final I. Across **2,817 distinct
key/origin variants**, 699 were invertible. **17,580 linear/Hill readings**,
including before/after header-key layers, produced no qualifying plaintext.
Singular direct-linear readings were diagnostics, not valid conventional Hill
keys. A separate I=0 appended-key control recovered **all 1,200 letters**.
This is not an exhaustive unknown-key Hill6 search.

### The final seven full groups as coordinates

A further **96,480 readings** treated the seven groups as scalar addresses or
row/group/letter fields into the preceding 145 rows. The tested variants
included bases 26/27/32/36, zero-/one-based conventions, signed/wrapped I=0,
reversed readouts, header-key layers, letter/group/physical-column domains,
and optionally using the final I as an external zero coordinate. All 120
five-digit assignments to two row digits, two group digits and one letter
digit were covered in the bounded modulo-address family.

No seven-/eight-letter dictionary or clue-target result was found, and the
longer group-address readings remained gibberish. Physical spaces were retained
and cells outside a printed row stayed distinguishable from real spaces.
Independent scalar and field-address controls recovered **OUTLIER** exactly.
These exploratory modulo reductions are not evidence that a book cipher was
intended; self-references into the final key line and arbitrary addressing
schemes were not covered.

## Prior continuation: generator recovery and assumption checks

**No new plaintext or verified construction.** TRACT remains a conditional
candidate; the new tests do not establish it as the intended answer.

### Could a random generator reveal planted text?

A new, seed-independent MT19937 analysis built linear constraints from the
high bits implied by letters sampled into 26 equal bins. Two fixed word-use
models were tested: one 32-bit word per scaled letter, and the two-word floating
model used by Python's `random.choices` / `int(random()*26)`.

The raw stream and BLOOM/BLPNM/BLNPM decryptions all failed the unmodified-prefix
checks. To avoid assuming that the planted payload must be near the known
fragment, the search also allowed a **1,000-letter erasure** at 11 overlapping
positions spanning the whole body. Raw, BLOOM and BLPNM inputs × two draw models
× 11 erasures gave **66 further models**. None was consistent with the necessary
MT equations, even when the erased letters were completely unconstrained.

Controls were checked against native Python MT state. In the two-word model,
7,550 training letters predicted all **1,186 later control letters** exactly.
A separate masked-block test recovered **all 1,000 original control letters**
inside the erasure, despite a planted 20-letter replacement there. The first
control character remained underdetermined by the retained high bits. The
one-word controls passed the necessary-equation checks but were underidentified;
they did **not** fully recover their control text.

A further **24 models** tested eight fixed letter/indentation draw schedules
on the 7,550-letter prefix: one- or two-word letter draws, one-word top-three-bit
or two-word float indentation draws, and indentation immediately before or
after each row. Raw, BLOOM and BLPNM inputs all failed these checks too. Native
state-equation controls passed every configuration; the two-word-letter
controls also predicted all later control letters and indentation values.
One-word-letter controls remained underidentified.

These negatives cover only the specified draw schedules and input versions.
They do **not** exclude `random.choice` / `randrange` rejection sampling,
arbitrary additional/interleaved calls, other random generators, or multiple
widely separated modified regions in the erasure test. The fixed-interleaving
prefix tests also do not exclude an early planted alteration. In particular,
Python's `randrange(8)` is not equivalent to a single `getrandbits(3)` call.
That earlier round recovered no letter-stream state or seed. The later exact
seed4300 indentation match above is a separate result.

### How robust is the I=0 extraction?

The final row actually contains two I's: an internal I at physical column **19**
and the final singleton at **48**. Testing both exposes a distinction:

- With the final singleton as zero, **TRACT** remains the sole dictionary hit
  under BLPNM in the strict five-column block immediately left of zero.
- With column 19 as zero, **FRODO** returns under BLOOM only for the looser
  nearest-complete-group rule. It is **not** adjacent: five intervening columns
  include part of the next group. This does not give FRODO the same strict fit.
- Holding the known row-127/group-8 candidate and final guide fixed, randomly
  reassigning the 145 observed full-row indentation values would produce the
  exact TRACT abutment in **20/145 = 13.79%** of assignments. It would remain
  the nearest complete group on the left in **39/145 = 26.90%**.

The last calculation measures the incremental role of indentation after the
word/group is known, not the overall chance of finding that word and position.
The final singleton is more strongly distinguished typographically than the
internal I, but its use as zero is still a hypothesis. No statistical row
outlier is established by these lexical selectors.

### Are FRODO and TRACT unusually close?

The two earlier word hits are six groups, or 30 letters, apart. A separate
**100,000-trial** calibration shuffled letters within their five group phases,
using the same 9,357-entry five-letter dictionary and only the literal keys
BLOOM, BLPNM and BLNPM at phase 0 with ordinary subtraction.

- **4,161/100,000 = 4.161%** produced some cross-key dictionary-word pair in
  distinct groups at least as close as the observed pair.
- Restricting the comparison to BLOOM/BLPNM gave **1.383%**.
- Of the 2,569 trials with exactly the observed per-key match counts `[1,1,0]`,
  20 had a pair as close: about **0.779%**.

These are different conditional comparisons, not interchangeable probabilities.
The most favorable number should not be selected as the headline. Clustering
was examined after observing the words, and this calculation is neither a
global correction for all earlier searches nor independent of the short-window
score test. The two rates must not be multiplied to claim a solution.

### Short-fragment calibration with the key search included

A new sweep used all **51** zero-sum ±1 variations of BLOOM, five phases and


| Search scope | Shuffled trials with a maximum at least as high as observed |

### “CONTRACT” still requires unsupported changes

Making the exact fragment into `CONTRACTCOMEDUEDATEZ` would require changing
source **ZHSVZ → ZHRBZ**: S→R and V→B, not the one-step +1/−1 pair suggested by
“one given / one taken.” The two 20-letter ciphertext spans have longest common
subsequence length 18, so their insertion/deletion distance is **4**. One
insertion and one deletion cannot supply that correction either. The original
images agree with the uncorrected transcript.

The scanner now has an explicit short-span path, so this fragment is no longer
hidden behind its default 24-letter prose gate:

```console
python3 scripts/stego_channel_scan.py /path/to/nachtlorn_transcript.txt --key-window BLPNM --group-key BLPNM
```

`--key-window-size` defaults to 20. It scans every letter offset, keeps key
phase across spaces/rows, and labels threshold hits as **candidates**, with a
multiple-testing warning. All **18 scanner tests** pass, including unaligned
short spans, row/group crossings, the final possible window, exact-length
inputs, invalid options and unchanged default prose gating.

## Other concrete findings

- The unmodified title **NACHTLORN** has determinant **18 modulo 26** when used
