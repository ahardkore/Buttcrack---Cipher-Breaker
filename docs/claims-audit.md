# Audit of the documented claims, 2026-09-30

The `kryptos/` records were audited first ([`kryptos/AUDIT.md`](../kryptos/AUDIT.md));
this is the same exercise applied to the claims the README and these docs make
about the solver. Each one was re-run rather than re-read. Three were wrong,
and two of those were wrong because of bugs rather than typos.

Re-measure the numbers with:

```console
$ python3 scripts/bench_claims.py --all       # gate, cribs, M-94, Kryptos
$ python3 examples/generate.py --check        # the sixteen example puzzles
$ python3 kryptos/verify_pk_records.py        # the PK records
```

## Corrected

**PK3 takes about 35 seconds, not 5.** Three consecutive runs: 34.8, 35.5,
35.7 s. The key it recovers really is `ORDINATE` + `PENTIMENTO`, but the report
led with the raw wheel integers, so the words were buried behind
`wheels=[[5, 1, 10, ...]]`. The key dict now leads with the words.

**The other PK timings had drifted** in the other direction: PK1 is ~8 s
against a documented ~15, PK2 ~15 s against ~29. The table now says what this
machine measures and names the conditions, because the timings are
hardware-dependent even when the solved/not-solved column is not.

**The sum-clock crib table was optimistic.** Re-measured over five trials per
crib length with a fixed seed and a stated sample: 12 letters 0 of 5, 14
letters 0 of 5, 16 letters 2 of 5, 19 letters 5 of 5. The published table
claimed 2 of 4 at fourteen and 4 of 4 at sixteen. The 19-letter row improved
in the other direction, from "0.1 s" to under ten milliseconds, after the bug
below.

**The M-94 claim was conservative**, not optimistic: 6 of 6 at 250 letters in
20 s, against a documented 2-in-3.

**`examples/README.md` said fifteen puzzles.** There are sixteen.

**The wiki was two articles short and quoted a stale count.** The committed
pages said "searches 50 ciphers" and had no article for `keyed_hill`, because
they are generated by hand and had not been regenerated. The count now comes
from the registry, so the number in the copy cannot drift from the tool again.

## Three bugs the audit turned up

**A near-identity decode derailed a layered puzzle.** `13-three-layers.txt`
(base64 over hex over repeating-key XOR) was answered with 67 letters of
nonsense at confidence 0.75 — a false positive, reported as SOLVED. The hex
layer decodes to 391 bytes of XOR output that happen to contain two `=XX`
sequences, which was enough for the quoted-printable layer to claim it. The
"decode" changed five bytes, which is 1% of the payload and all of the XOR
alignment. Quoted-printable now requires the escapes to be dense enough to be
real (one per sixty characters), and the puzzle solves in 0.9 s at confidence
1.00.

**The last scheduling phase was being handed zero seconds.** On
`16-m94.txt` the engine spent its budget and reported a 0.53-confidence chain,
while the M-94 attack — which cracks that ciphertext on its own in six
seconds — was offered a "0s slice", with 43 seconds of budget still unspent.
Two causes: the identifier's hypotheses were the only input to scheduling at
the root, so the M-94's own detector (0.77 on that text) was never consulted;
and proportional sharing leaves nothing for the last phase. Self-assessments
are now blended in at the root as they already were deeper in the search,
damped so they cannot outrank evidence from the text itself, and a
well-evidenced expensive attack is guaranteed a workable slice instead of its
arithmetic share of the dregs. The puzzle solves at confidence 1.00.

**Attribution depended on scheduling order.** The search stops at the first
certain answer, so when two ciphers produce the same plaintext the one that
runs first takes the credit and the other never runs at all. Changing the
scheduling above was enough to make a ROT13 ciphertext report as `caesar`.
Equivalent spellings now share their evidence and the tie breaks on a declared
preference order, so a 13-shift is ROT13 and a reciprocal alphabet is Atbash
regardless of which attack the scheduler reaches first.

With those fixed, `examples/generate.py --check` goes from 14 of 16 to 16 of
16, and the self-test from 143 to 144 of 144.
