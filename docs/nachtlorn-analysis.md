## 3. Statistical evidence and its limits

| Test | Observed | Uniform baseline |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Index of coincidence (IC) | 0.03855 | 0.03846 |
| Monogram χ², 25 degrees of freedom | 45.3 | Mean 25 |
| Digram χ² | 715.8 | Approximately 675 |
| Differential IC, shifts 1–399 | 0.03839–0.03859 | Approximately 0.03846 |
| Best column IC, five-letter grouping | 0.0386 | Approximately 0.03846 |
| Best column IC, 60-letter lines | 0.0433 | Length-dependent sampling variation |

Ordinary English has IC around 0.0667. These measurements disfavor a simple
monoalphabetic substitution or a pure transposition of ordinary English. They
also provide no positive evidence for a short repeating Vigenère key.

They do **not** exclude every polyalphabetic cipher, autokey construction,
running key, keyed steganographic extraction, or modern encryption scheme.
Differencing ciphertext at a repeating key's period cancels the key, but leaves
**differences of plaintext letters**, not plaintext itself. Those differences do
not generally have English's IC of 0.0667. An earlier version of this analysis
incorrectly treated that value as a decisive requirement.

The scanner's original “IC over lags” calculation was actually IC of progressively
shorter suffixes, `stream[k:]`; it was not a periodicity test. Its report now names
that measurement correctly. Flat suffix IC is not evidence against a repeating
key.

`E` occurs 412 times (about +4.2 standard deviations against a fixed uniform
single-letter model). This warrants noting, but is not a reliable fingerprint of
an LLM, a particular generator, or an intended outlier. Tests chosen after looking
at the data require caution about multiple comparisons.
